How To TAKE Customers from Just looking

Eight graphics, down from the ten in the brief: seven placed in the newsletter plus one optional key. All are drawn to scale and use only the newsletter's own words. The one blocking error in the copy, the opening 97% line, has been fixed (flag 1 below).

Read this first: the content check

I checked every number. The pyramid math holds: 3 + 17 + 20 + 60 = 100, 17 + 20 = 37, 37 + 60 = 97. The problems are in the sentences that use those numbers.

1 · Fixed. The opening line now agrees with the framework.
Was: 1st To Reach 97% who're ready to buy now (but could be very soon)
Now: 1st To Reach 97% who're NOT ready to buy now (but could be very soon)
Only the 3% are Buying Now, so the 97% are the people who aren't. The only change is the added word "NOT". It's in capitals to match how the copy stresses other words ("TAKE", "SHUT UP"). M1 can now go next to this line.
2 · Three different return figures.
put 1$ in which generate $2, $5, $10, $50 or even $200 back, then putting $1 in & getting $3 back, then put in a dollar & buy a $5 or a $10 note. They don't contradict each other, but $3 isn't in the first list. The graphic (H3) uses only the first set, word for word, as a range on a log scale with no single value highlighted. It shows a range, not a promised return.
3 · Unsupported claims. None of these appear in a graphic.
They're virtually 99% ready to buy (99% is a figure of speech, not a segment of the pyramid). smashes these woeful returns on investment (it compares against returns the text never states, so a paragraph seems to be missing). Advertising is an Investment that makes more money than anything else. Period.
4 · "Right away" vs. "up the pyramid faster".
shift … 97% … to becoming your customer right away conflicts with move the 97% of potential Customers up the pyramid faster. Every graphic shows gradual movement, which matches most of the copy.
5 · Naming and stage words.
The text uses both THE LARGE MARKET FORMULA and The LARGER MARKET FORMULA. The graphics use the heading's spelling. The copy's stages are Attract / Educates / Nurtures / Gets prospects to act. The brief asked for ATTRACT / EDUCATE / NURTURE / ACT, so H2 uses the brief's labels. If the author wants the exact copy wording, change them.
6 · Attribution risk.
The 3 / 17 / 20 / 60 split, "HVCO", "The Godfather Strategy" and "The HALO STRATEGY" closely match Sabri Suby's Sell Like Crazy. I'm fairly confident of this but haven't checked it against the book. A polished, branded infographic makes a borrowed framework look like the author's own. Add a credit line under H1 or check the source before publishing.

Part 1 · Visual strategy

The structural problem the graphics have to solve

The newsletter's argument runs backwards. It refers to 97% and moving them up the pyramid in the first lines, but doesn't define the pyramid until the last third. The graphics can't reorder locked copy. They can give the reader the model early: a small pyramid key at the opening (optional) and the full formula as the strongest graphic where the copy finally defines it.

Second problem: the copy uses two metaphors that point in opposite directions. In the pyramid, buyers are at the top and readers move up. In a funnel, buyers are at the bottom. If both are drawn vertically, the reader has to flip the picture in their head. So the vertical axis is reserved for the market pyramid, where up means closer to buying. Every process (Attract → Act, the funnel, the advertising system) runs left to right.

Direction

  • Style: flat editorial information design, like a financial-press chart, not a SaaS landing page. Hairline leaders, bracket annotations, and numbers set as the main visual element.
  • Density: four hero graphics, three supporting, and one optional micro key. The brief listed ten. Three of them (cold traffic system, advertising → profit, advertising as a system) were the same pipeline drawn three times, so they're merged into one hero (H3). The vending machine is cut because H3 already shows the $1-in, return-out idea, and a vending machine drawing tends toward clip-art.
  • Typography: Bricolage Grotesque (heavy, for numbers and key ideas), IBM Plex Sans (labels), IBM Plex Mono uppercase (eyebrows, structural notes, and short verbatim quotes from the copy). Three roles, two families.
  • Color: one cobalt accent with tonal steps, on cool paper with blue-black ink. Encoding: ink = the crowded 3%, cobalt = the 37% opportunity and progress toward action, hatched grey = the 60% not problem aware. Amber is used only for money ($1 in, returns back). No rainbow categories.
  • 3D: none. Any perspective distorts the areas, and the pyramid's credibility depends on the areas being honest. 3D also dates quickly and pushes the look toward generic SaaS.
paper ink · 3% accent · 17% accent-mid · 20% hatch · 60% money only

Part 3 + 4 · Specifications and the finished graphics

Each graphic below is the production file: vector SVG built from computed geometry, plus a 2× PNG for email. Source, SVG and PNG files are in newsletter-visuals/ on the branch.

H1 · The Large Market Formula

Placement
After Let's understand THE LARGE MARKET FORMULA. It shows the same data as the table, so the table can stay as accessible text below it or be removed. That's the author's call.
Objective
Within 3 seconds: the buying group is tiny, the 37% above the base is the opportunity, and 97% of the market is outside "buying now".
Composition
Pyramid on the left two-thirds; labels in a right column joined by dotted leaders; 37% bracket on the left; full-width 100-unit strip at the foot that restates 3% vs 97% as a straight line.
Hierarchy
1 · 3% tip in solid ink (smallest area, darkest) → 2 · 37% bracket in accent → 3 · 97% under the strip → 4 · segment names.
Text
Only copy terms: THE LARGE MARKET FORMULA, Pyramid level · Share, the four segment names exactly as written, at any given time 3% of people are in buying mode!. Added structural label: "Area to scale".
Why area-true
With equal-height bands, the 3% tip would look like about 25%, which is the failure the brief warned about. Here the 3% band takes 17% of the height but exactly 3% of the area. The strip at the foot covers readers who judge by height.
Don't
Don't use equal-height tiers, 3D or perspective, or the 99% figure. Don't use the brief's phrase "closer to buying" as a label, because the copy doesn't say it.

H2 · Attract → Educate → Nurture → Act

Placement
After ④ Gets prospects to act.
Objective
The four stages read as one movement from cold to ready, not four boxes.
Composition
One horizontal track with an arrowhead; four nodes spaced evenly; stage word above, copy numeral above that; end labels below the track.
Encoding
Two cues show momentum: node radius 12 → 30 and fill from outline to solid accent. ACT is the only word set in accent.
Text
The Key to Install them is -, ①–④, stage words (see flag 5), cold traffic, pre qualified.
Don't
Don't use rounded rectangles, icons per stage, or a circular loop. The copy describes a one-way path, not a cycle.

H3 · Advertising → profit system

Placement
After You need a System you can put 1$ in which generate $2, $5, $10, $50 or even $200 back.
Objective
One picture that joins the stages the copy spreads across four sections: traffic sources, HVCO education, auto-pilot nurture, conversion, profit, and reinvestment.
Composition
$1 input (amber) → seven-station line → vertical return scale (amber). Bracketed zones above the line use copy phrases. A dashed reinvestment loop below returns to the input.
Hierarchy
$1 → the line → the return range → zone labels → sub-labels.
Text
All from copy: Automated Lead generation and Client Conversion System, that turns Advertising into profit, put 1$ in, Your System - not you, google · facebook, HVCO, on auto pilot, Customer, back, or even, invest as much as your cash flows allows.
Don't
Don't put "$200" large on its own, draw a bar chart of returns (it reads as a result), add $3 to the scale, or use a vending machine.

H4 · The Halo Strategy

Placement
After ⑤ The stuff they're thinking but not telling anyone.
Honest weakness
This is the weakest hero because it repeats a five-item list. It earns its place with one idea the list can't show: the item that matters most is the one hidden closest to the customer. If space is tight, use it at half width.
Composition
Concentric rings: ink core CUSTOMER, solid accent ring ⑤ with text set along the arc, four equal pale quadrants ①–④ (equal because the copy doesn't rank them), and a dotted outer halo.
Text
The HALO STRATEGY, YOU MUST KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER INTIMATELY!, the five items as written, Customer's deepest & most on the lower arc.
Don't
Don't use brains, silhouettes, thought bubbles or icebergs.

S1 · The 3% competition problem

Placement
After you're never going to make much money.
Composition
The same area-true pyramid split 3 / 97. Nine identical square markers (businesses) all aim at the tip, and nothing aims at the hatched 97%. being Ignored is from the copy.
Don't
Don't draw cartoon people, logos, or a "you" marker. The copy doesn't say where the reader stands.

S2 · The 37% opportunity

Placement
After They are either gathering Info (17%) or problem Aware (20%).
Composition
The same four segments, laid out as a straight 100-unit strip (honest by length). A bracket marks 17% + 20%, with the copy's own line I'm kinda thirsty - What Should I drink? above it.
Don't
Don't write equation notation ("17 + 20 = 37") in display type. That's the worksheet look the brief wanted to avoid.

S3 · Sales funnel

Placement
After the two Sales funnel sentences.
Composition
Horizontal on purpose (see Part 1), so it doesn't fight the pyramid's direction. Three tapering segments with fill that deepens toward Customer.
Don't
Don't add conversion rates. The copy has none.

M1 · Pyramid key (optional)

Final quality pass

  • Is each graphic necessary? Two from the brief were cut and three were merged into one. S1 is the first to drop if needed.
  • Does any graphic add information? No. Every word on the graphics is copy text, except two structural labels ("Area to scale", stage numerals).
  • Does any graphic make a weak claim look stronger? The return range is shown as a range, the 99% figure and "more money than anything else" aren't drawn, and the opening-line contradiction is flagged rather than illustrated.
  • Do they look like one system? Same paper, ink, accent steps, hatch, three type roles, dotted leaders and bracket style throughout.
